Sequel Tea — Business Analytics & Dashboard

Systems Optimization Club (Cal Poly SLO) · Project Lead · Team of 5

Sequel Tea, a boba tea shop launching a new on-campus store, needed a way to actually see how the business was performing — instead of retyping sales numbers into spreadsheets by hand every day. I led a 5-person student consulting team to design and build a live analytics dashboard that pulls directly from the store's point-of-sale systems.

The problem

Sales and financial data came from two disconnected sources — Grubhub (online orders) and Micros (in-store POS) — and had to be manually entered and charted, taking roughly 10 minutes per update with limited, inconsistent visuals and no automated reporting.

What I built (with my team)

As project lead and primary client contact, I coordinated requirements gathering and stakeholder meetings with Sequel Tea, then directed the design of:

  • An automated ETL data pipeline (Google Apps Script) that detects, parses, and loads Grubhub and Micros CSV exports every night — with error handling that routes bad files to a review folder instead of silently failing.
  • Three live Google Data Studio dashboards: Sales (channel mix, daily/weekly trends, category breakdowns), Financial (revenue, COGS %, labor %, revenue per labor hour), and Inventory (ingredient usage, week-over-week comparisons, restocking signals).
  • A COGS and ingredient-consumption calculator that derives material costs and usage straight from sales data and recipes, instead of requiring manual tracking.
  • A 25-page user guide documenting how to operate, maintain, and troubleshoot the entire system for non-technical staff.

Results

98%reduction in data-processing time
~10 secvs. ~10 min manual entry, per update
3live dashboards shipped (sales, financial, inventory)
“More than just a time difference, because the data in those 10 minutes is nowhere near as comprehensive.” — Justin Chan, Sequel Tea

Beyond the time savings, the dashboards gave ownership faster access to decision-relevant metrics for staffing, inventory, and pricing, and set up the data pipeline to scale as Sequel Tea expands to new locations.

What I learned

Scoping a project so it stays achievable and genuinely useful matters more than adding features; tailoring technical explanations to a non-technical audience (and scheduling regular client check-ins) made the difference between a dashboard that looked good and one the client actually adopted.

Tools: Google Data Studio · Google Sheets · Apps Script (JavaScript) · Grubhub & Micros POS data
